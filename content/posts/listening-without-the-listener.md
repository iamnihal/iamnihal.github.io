Picture two people sitting across from each other, having a conversation. On the surface, they are talking. Underneath, neither is actually listening.

In the world of constant stimulation, our brain is so heavily overloaded with dopamine that giving our undivided attention feels almost tiring. Without even realising, there’s always a flux of constant mental gymnastics going inside our brain while the other person is speaking. Instead of actually listening to the speaker, we drift away with our thoughts. Instead of listening attentively without any psychological division, we simply wait for them to pause so we can jump in with the exact thought we've been rehearsing in our heads the entire time.

Even if we do manage to listen attentively, we rarely hear what is actually being said. We filter all sensory signals through our own [**"perception box”**](https://bigthink.com/perception-box/) which is nothing but a narrow frame built from our past experiences, prejudices, and unexamined beliefs. Because our sense of **self** (*ego*) is so tightly wrapped up around our opinions, beliefs and what not, any perspective that challenges our box feels like a threat. Our sense of self doesn’t want to be disrupted by new perspectives that are outside of our perception ([**conditioning**](https://nihalchoudhary.in/posts/2025/10/the-untouched-mind/)) that we have built throughout our lives. Our ego steps in to judge, defend, or dismiss before we ever truly understand. And surprisingly, it all happens unconsciously. This is how our brain has evolved to quickly assess a particular piece of information and come up with an answer as soon as possible from our limited knowledge.

Truly listening, or listening without judgement is not a communication skill or some trick. I think it’s a by-product of [**self-awareness**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-awareness).

When you objectively begin noticing your own mental noise, the frameworks that you’ve been unconsciously building and curating with ideas and beliefs that are not your own, and actively start questioning the content and the boundaries of your perception box, something shifts. You'll stop trying to protect your ego and start approaching conversations with genuine curiosity. The curiosity and interest to know a human being will not be forced. It will be choiceless. A [**choiceless awareness**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choiceless_awareness) that happens naturally without any explicit effort or willpower. You’ll stop judging yourself, and people around you.

At that point, the inner chatter quiets down. And it's not only limited to sensory perception, it encompasses every aspect of our life.

As [JK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiddu_Krishnamurti) said:

> **"The ability to observe without evaluating is the highest form of intelligence."**

When that happens, the division between the listener and the act of listening simply disappears.
